# Content plan — what's missing, and why it matters

The site is structurally complete. It reads generic because it describes the
business **in the abstract**: no dates, no places, no names, no faces, no
numbers. Real company sites are specific. Every gap below is a specificity gap.

Ordered by what it buys you, not by how hard it is.

---

## 1. Facts a lender checks and cannot currently find

A loan officer verifying MILLZ Global Solutions LLC will look for four things
before anything else. The site answers none of them today.

### 1.1 Formation date and entity number — the single cheapest credibility win

There is **not one date anywhere on this site.** No founding year, no "operating
since". To someone checking whether this is a real company, that reads as
evasive even though it isn't.

Add a plain company-details block:

> **MILLZ Global Solutions LLC**
> Minnesota limited liability company
> Formed: `[month, year]`
> MN business file number: `[number]`
> Registered agent: `[name]`

This is public record at the Minnesota Secretary of State, so publishing it costs
nothing and it is independently verifiable — which is exactly why it works.
**Nothing else on this site will do more for the loan application.**

*Needed from you:* formation date, file number, registered agent name.

### 1.2 A named human

The site never says who runs the company. The founder section is an explicit
placeholder, and no name appears anywhere — not in the footer, not on contact.
A business with no named person behind it is the pattern lenders are trained to
be suspicious of.

*Needed from you:* the founder's name as it should be published, and the
2–4 paragraphs the About section is already built to receive.

### 1.3 What the company owns

The apparel division links to a placeholder URL. Once it points somewhere real,
it becomes evidence of a second operating business. Right now it is an assertion.

*Needed from you:* the apparel site URL, and confirmation the storefront is live.

### 1.4 A privacy policy — currently a genuine legal gap

The contact form collects name, email, phone and a message, and there is **no
privacy policy anywhere on the site.** That is a real compliance problem, not a
nice-to-have: you are collecting personal data with no stated basis, retention
period, or contact for deletion requests.

Add `/privacy` and `/terms`, linked from the footer. I can draft both from the
actual data flow (form → Resend → your inbox), and you have a lawyer check them.

## 2. The question every prospective client asks, unanswered

### 2.1 Where, physically, does training happen?

The site says "Mankato and southern Minnesota". That is the **first** thing a
prospective client wants to know and the site does not answer it:

- Which gym, or do you have your own space?
- Do you travel to clients? Home visits? Their gym?
- Outdoor sessions in summer?
- Is there parking?

*Needed from you:* the actual arrangement. If it is "my garage gym in North
Mankato" or "I meet clients at Anytime Fitness on Adams Street", say so — that
specificity is worth more than any amount of polish.

### 2.2 Named towns, not a region

"Southern Minnesota" is invisible to local search. Listing real towns is how
someone in Eagle Lake finds you:

> North Mankato · St. Peter · Eagle Lake · Lake Crystal · Nicollet · Le Sueur ·
> Madison Lake · Skyline

*Needed from you:* how far you will actually travel, so the list is honest.

### 2.3 A real way to book

There is a contact form and nothing else. An operating training business almost
always has a scheduling link — Calendly, Acuity, Square Appointments. Adding one
does two things: it converts far better than a form, and a live booking calendar
is itself evidence the business is trading.

*Needed from you:* whether you use a scheduler, or want one set up.

### 2.4 Your approach, in your words

The training page explains formats and prices but never says **how you train or
what you believe about it.** That is the only thing that separates you from a
gym membership, and it cannot be written by anyone but you.

Two or three paragraphs. Not marketing — how you actually work with someone who
walks in having never trained.

## 3. Proof the business is alive

### 3.1 You already have social proof and it is not on the site

`customer_01.jpg` carries a **`@toughclothin` TikTok watermark** — an existing
audience, posting real customers in real product, in what looks like a retail
space. None of it is linked from the site.

A live social feed is one of the strongest "this is a real operating business"
signals available, and it costs nothing. Link the accounts in the footer.

*Needed from you:* the handles you want public (TikTok, Instagram, Facebook), and
confirmation the retail location in that photo is somewhere you actually sell.

### 3.2 Eight product photographs, none used

The apparel division shows one image. You have eight garment mockups sitting
unused — hoodies in black, red and white, shorts, pants, a blue shirt.

The parent site does not need a shop, but a small product strip in the apparel
block would make that division look real rather than described. **These files are
2.4–5.7 MB each and must be resized before use** — they are print-resolution.

### 3.3 Testimonials

The component is built and hidden. One real, attributable quote from a training
client changes the page more than any design change. Nothing invented.

*Needed from you:* one or two clients willing to be quoted by name.

## 4. Structural additions worth making

| Add | Why |
|---|---|
| **`/about`** — a real page, not just a home section | Somewhere to put formation details, the founder story, and the origin of the company. Currently there is nowhere for a lender to land. |
| **`/privacy` and `/terms`** | Legal requirement given the form. See 1.4. |
| **Service-area section on `/training`** | Local SEO, and it answers 2.1 and 2.2. |
| **FAQ on the home page** | Parent-level questions ("are these separate businesses?", "who do I contact about wholesale?") that the training FAQ does not cover. |
| **Google Business Profile** | Not on the site, but the highest-leverage thing for a local trainer. Free, and it feeds the map pack. |

Deliberately **not** recommending a blog or news feed. An empty or stale one
actively damages credibility, and it is a commitment to keep writing.

2. **Founder name + portrait + 2–4 paragraphs** in their own words.
3. **Where training physically happens**, and how far you travel.
4. **Apparel site URL**, and confirmation it is live.
5. **Real phone number** (the current one is a 555 placeholder).
6. **Social handles** you want public.
7. **Training photography** — still the biggest visual gap.
8. **Confirm the pricing table** — all seven rows are my estimates.
9. **Your training approach**, 2–3 paragraphs.
10. **One or two testimonials**, with permission.

Send 1, 2, 3 and 5 and the site stops reading like a template.
